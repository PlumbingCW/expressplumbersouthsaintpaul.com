When a clogged drain turns into an emergency

Most clogs are a nuisance. A few are urgent, and the difference usually shows up within an hour.

  • Water coming up in a basement floor drain or tub when you run the washer means the main line is blocked. Stop all water use in the house.
  • More than one fixture gurgling or draining slowly at the same time points to the main, not the sink trap.
  • Sewage smell or standing water near the drain is a health risk. Keep children and pets out of the room.
  • If the backup is in a rental or shared building, call the owner and us. The cleanup clock starts when water reaches finished floors.

What happens on a drain cleaning visit

We start by asking which fixtures are affected and what was running when it backed up. That alone narrows most jobs to a branch line or the main.

A small hand auger clears a tub or lavatory trap. A drum machine with a cutting head handles kitchen lines and 3 or 4 inch laundry stacks. When the cable keeps pulling back greasy or rooty material, we tell you a camera or hydro jetting will hold up better than another pass with a blade.

Cable work is quick, but we do not guess. If the line will not clear after a reasonable pass, you hear that at the cleanout and not on the invoice.

Why older South St. Paul homes clog more often

The city's 2040 planning documents put about 74 percent of homes in South St. Paul as built before 1970, and most of the older stock sits above the bluff west of Concord Street. Homes from that period often have cast iron or clay sewer lines, and both collect scale and tree roots over decades.

That does not mean your line is failing. It means a stubborn clog deserves a camera look before the third cleaning in two years. If the camera shows a crack or a sag, sewer line repair is a better spend than repeated cabling.

Is it your line or the city's?

The City of South St. Paul maintains the sewer main in the street. The line from your house to that main is yours. If every home on your block is backing up, call city Public Works at 651-554-3225 on weekdays or 911 after hours.

The city also asks residents to contact its Utilities Division before hiring a drain cleaner and to report it after a private line has been cleaned. We will point you to the right number and clean only the part that belongs to you.
