What 18 grains per gallon means for your house

Hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. The Minnesota Department of Health notes that water over about 7 grains per gallon, or 120 milligrams per liter, may benefit from softening. The city's reported figure is well above that line.

Because hardness can change over time and between homes, we recommend you test your tap rather than rely only on the citywide number. Ask us or the city's water utility, and keep the annual drinking water report handy. The city issues it each April.

A softener and a filter do different jobs.

Where hardness shows up first

Water heaters usually show hard water damage before anything else. Scale on elements and on tank bottoms raises energy use and shortens the life of the heater. See tankless water heaters for the units most sensitive to scale.

Dishwashers, faucet aerators and showerheads also clog with mineral crust. The first signs are white crust on fixtures, spots on glasses and soap that does not lather.

Rules and who can do the work

Minnesota's plumbing code, chapter 4714, governs how a softener connects to supply and drain. The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry issues water conditioning licenses, and plumbers install the water supply piping. Check credentials in the DLI lookup.

Softener settings, salt type and discharge matter, and we explain them in plain language so you can maintain the system.

Salt, maintenance and what to expect

A salt-based softener uses salt to recharge. Check the salt level every few weeks and keep it above the water line in the brine tank. Salt bridges, where a hard crust forms, can stop the unit from working, so break them up if you see one.

Softened water feels slippery on skin, which surprises some people. Most notice less scale, fewer spots and softer laundry. If you are on a sodium-restricted diet, ask your doctor about the sodium added by softening and use a separate drinking tap if advised.

What to do if your water looks or tastes wrong

Not every water problem is hardness.

  • Run a cold tap for several minutes and check again
  • Note color, smell and when it started
  • Ask the City of South St. Paul about recent main work or flushing
  • Do not drink water that smells like sewage or fuel
  • Call us if the problem is only in your house
