The hidden cost of tankless is water chemistry

The City of South St. Paul reports typical hardness of about 18 grains per gallon. That is hard water, and a tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages that scale can narrow further. Scale lowers output, triggers error codes and shortens the life of the unit.

Plan on a descaling flush on a schedule and consider a softener ahead of the heater. Our water softener and filtration page explains how softening protects the heat exchanger.

Is a tankless heater a fit for your home?

Run through this list before you choose one.

  • Your gas line can deliver the higher BTU load or you have the electric capacity
  • You have a path for new venting, which may differ from a tank's flue
  • You are willing to flush it on a schedule
  • Your household uses hot water in steady amounts, not many outlets at once
  • You plan to stay long enough to recover the higher first cost

Winter water and sizing

A tankless unit raises the temperature of whatever comes in. Incoming water in a Minnesota January is much colder than in July, so the same unit delivers less flow at your chosen temperature in winter. A unit sized only for summer will disappoint when two showers run in February.

We size against the cold-season case and ask how many fixtures you want running together. If the answer is more than a unit can cover, we say so and talk through a larger unit or a standard tank instead.
