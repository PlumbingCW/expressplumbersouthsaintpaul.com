Find the water source first

Waterproofing starts with a diagnosis. A wet basement can come from a floor crack, a cove joint where wall meets floor, a window well, a leaking pipe or high groundwater. Each has a different fix.

Before we recommend a system, we rule out the simple causes. Gutter downspouts that discharge at the foundation, soil that slopes toward the house and a failing sump discharge all cause wet basements without any foundation defect.

South St. Paul housing and basements

The city's 2040 planning documents put about 74 percent of homes in South St. Paul as built before 1970, and most of the older stock sits above the bluff west of Concord Street. Older foundations of block, stone or early poured concrete often leak at mortar joints or the cove joint.

Only about 107 housing units have been built in the city since 2000, so most basements have decades of settling and seasonal movement behind them. The 3.5 foot frost depth for Dakota County means soil around foundations freezes and thaws every year, and that cycle opens small cracks.

Before you spend on waterproofing

These low-cost steps solve many damp basements.

  • Extend downspouts several feet from the foundation
  • Check that soil slopes away from the house
  • Clean window wells and cover them
  • Test your sump pump and its discharge
  • Run a dehumidifier and watch the humidity reading

Humidity, mold and what to do after the water is gone

A basement that has been wet stays damp for a while. Run a dehumidifier and keep the humidity under about half of what the air can hold. Remove wet carpet and drywall that did not dry within a day or two, since mold can start in that time.

Mold testing and removal are a separate trade. We fix the water source and the plumbing. If you see widespread mold, ask a mold professional about removal, and call your insurer about the claim.
