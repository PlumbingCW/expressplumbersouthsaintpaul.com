Toilet overflowing right now

Fast action keeps this from becoming a ceiling repair downstairs.

  • Turn the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  • Lift the tank lid and press the flapper down or lift the float to stop the fill.
  • Do not flush again. Use towels at the base to hold water back.
  • If other fixtures are slow or gurgling, the main line may be blocked. Stop all water and call us.

The flange: the part most people never see

The toilet flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to. When it cracks, rusts or sits below the finished floor after a flooring change, the toilet rocks and the wax seal fails. Another wax ring will not hold until the flange is fixed.

We check the flange height and condition whenever we pull a toilet. Repair rings, extender kits and full flange replacements are all options, and we choose by the floor and the pipe below.

How a toilet replacement runs

A standard replacement follows these steps.

Shut and drain

We close the supply, flush out the tank and disconnect the line.

Pull and inspect

We lift the old toilet and check the flange and the floor for water damage.

Set the new toilet

A new seal, new bolts and a level, secure fit.

Test

We flush repeatedly and check the supply line and base for leaks.

A running toilet and your water bill

The City of South St. Paul runs its own municipal water utility, and a silent leak at the flapper shows up as a higher usage line on the bill. Toilets are one of the most common causes of unexplained usage.

A quick test is to add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leaking flapper. If the meter still moves after that is fixed, leak detection looks elsewhere.
