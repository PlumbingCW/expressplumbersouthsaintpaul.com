Signs your water heater needs help

These are the symptoms we hear about most, ordered by how fast you should act.

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank, which can mean the tank itself has failed
  • A rotten egg or gas smell near a gas-fired heater, which is a leave-first, call-second situation
  • Lukewarm water that never gets hot, often a failed element or thermostat on an electric unit
  • Rumbling or popping from the tank, a sign of mineral buildup on the bottom
  • Rust-colored hot water only, which can point to a corroded tank or anode rod

How we find the fault before we quote

We shut off the fuel or power, then test rather than guess. On an electric heater that means checking both elements and thermostats with a meter. On a gas heater it means the thermocouple, gas valve, venting and burner.

Many repairs are small. A bad thermostat or element is a fraction of the cost of a tank. A leak from the tank body is different, because a steel tank that leaks from the shell cannot be patched. We say so plainly, and then water heater replacement becomes the honest answer.

Many tank heaters last around a decade, though local water and maintenance change that.

Hard water is hard on tanks here

The City of South St. Paul reports that its municipal well water is typically 18 grains per gallon, or 307 parts per million. That is hard water. Minerals settle at the bottom of a tank, force gas burners to run longer and bake onto electric elements until they burn out.

A yearly flush slows that down, and an anode rod check tells us how much protection the tank has left. If you rent or just bought an older home, ask when the heater was last flushed. A softener on the cold line, covered on our water softener and filtration page, protects the new tank as well.

Do this first

Water is leaking from the heater right now

A leaking tank can put gallons on the floor in an hour, and basements here often have a floor drain nearby that is not as clear as it looks.

  • Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the heater.
  • Switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot or off.
  • Open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure if the relief valve is discharging.
  • Move anything stored on the floor and call the 24/7 line for a same-day or after-hours visit.
