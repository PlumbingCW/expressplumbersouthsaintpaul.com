Replacement day: what the visit looks like

After we confirm the old tank cannot be repaired, we drain it, disconnect the water, gas or power, and carry it out. We then set the new heater, connect the supply lines and bring the venting, gas connection or electrical hookup up to what the plumbing code needs.

Minnesota's plumbing code is Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and it requires a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe. We install that, check for an expansion tank where the home has a closed water system, and test everything before we leave. The city lists water heaters among the permits available through its online system, and we tell you what applies to your job.

Tank, high-efficiency tank or tankless

No single type wins. The right one depends on how many people use hot water and what the house already has.

TypeBest fitWatch for
Standard gas or electric tankMost households, lowest first costStandby heat loss and a shorter lifespan in hard water
High-efficiency tankLarger families, lower fuel useHigher first cost and sometimes new venting
TanklessSmaller households, space limitsDescaling needs and gas line sizing, see tankless water heaters

We size by household and peak use, not by the biggest tank that fits.

Why South St. Paul water changes the choice

The city draws from municipal wells and reports hardness around 18 grains per gallon. In hard water a new tank still collects scale, but a sacrificial anode rod, a yearly flush and a softener on the cold side stretch its life.

If your old tank failed early, hard water is a likely cause. We will tell you if the age and condition of the old unit point to scale or to a defect. That is part of why we recommend pairing a replacement with a look at softener options.

Do this first

Cold showers and water on the floor: what to do first

A failed tank is rarely a gradual problem. It leaks or goes cold, and either way it needs a decision quickly.

  • Shut off the cold water above the heater and the fuel or power.
  • Protect the floor and finished walls. Basements with carpet tiles soak up water fast.
  • Call the 24/7 line. We schedule same-day replacement when stock and access allow.
  • Keep one hot-water-dependent task for later, like laundry, until the new heater is running.

What decides the price of a replacement

A fair quote comes from the home, not a rate card.

Heater type and sizeA 40 gallon tank, a 75 gallon tank and a tankless unit sit at very different price points.
Fuel and ventingA change from standard to high-efficiency venting adds pipe and labor.
Code upgradesRelief valve piping, an expansion tank or a drain pan may be required.
Access and removalStairs, a tight closet and carrying out the old unit add time.
TimingSame-day and after-hours replacements carry a call-out rate that we quote up front.

Insurance rarely covers a tank that wore out. If a sudden burst caused water damage, ask your carrier about the damage, not the heater.
