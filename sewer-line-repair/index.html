How we decide what needs repair

A cleaned line that clogs again, a bad smell in the yard or a sinkhole-like dip over the pipe all point to damage. We run a camera from the cleanout to the main and note the location of each defect by distance from the entry.

That video answers three questions. Is the pipe cracked, offset or collapsed? Are roots at the joints, or has the pipe sagged and holds water? And how much of the run is bad? Short problems get spot repairs. Long problems may call for lining or replacement.

Raw sewage in the house: what to do first

Treat any sewage backup as a health hazard, not a cleanup task.

  • Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the room.
  • Avoid touching the water. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter.
  • Call the 24/7 line. We book same-day appointments when available and after-hours dispatch for active backups.

Who owns which part of the sewer in South St. Paul

The City of South St. Paul maintains the sewer main in the street, and the private line from the building to the main is the property owner's job. If a camera shows the break in the public main, we stop and point you to the city Utilities Division.

The city also runs a Time of Sale program, and you can call 651-554-3220 to ask what applies when you list a home. If you are buying an older house, our sewer camera inspection shows what you would own.

Frost, soil and older lines

Minnesota's frost depth rule for Dakota County is 3.5 feet, and buried water piping has to sit at least 12 inches below local frost depth. Sewer lines sit deeper than that in most cases, but the freeze and thaw cycle still stresses older clay and cast iron joints.

In South St. Paul, much of the older housing stock above the bluff dates from before 1970, and many lines in that age range show joint wear or root intrusion. Often a hydro jetting visit buys time, and lining ends the cycle.

Depth and locationA line under a driveway, a deck or a mature tree costs more to reach.
Repair methodLining, bursting and trench repair carry different materials and equipment.
Surface restorationLawn, concrete and landscaping repair is part of the plan.
Permits and locatesA plumbing permit and a 811 locate are part of the timeline.

Insurance seldom pays for the pipe itself, because wear and roots are excluded. Water damage from a backup may be covered if you have a sewer backup endorsement.
