No heat in a hard freeze

A cold house is a plumbing risk, not just a comfort problem.

  • Check the thermostat, the power switch at the boiler and the breaker.
  • Look at the pressure gauge. Very low pressure can shut a boiler down.
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls and let a trickle run from a faucet on the coldest wall.
  • Call the 24/7 line. We book same-day appointments when available and after-hours dispatch for no-heat calls.

Why boilers matter in older South St. Paul homes

About 74 percent of South St. Paul homes were built before 1970, according to the city's 2040 planning documents, and many older houses still heat with hot water boilers and cast iron radiators. These systems last a long time, but their valves, pumps and expansion tanks wear out.

A cold night can expose a weak spot. The pressure relief valve, the circulator pump and the thermostat are common failures. Because the same plumbing carries water through the house, a stalled boiler can freeze pipes, which is when burst pipe repair becomes the second call.

Gas, carbon monoxide and rules

Xcel Energy supplies natural gas in South St. Paul. If you smell gas, leave the house, do not use switches or phones inside and call Xcel at 1-800-895-2999 or 911 from outside. Install carbon monoxide detectors near sleeping areas.

Minnesota exempts boilers in residential buildings of up to five families from state boiler inspection. That exemption removes the inspection, not the need for a competent repair. Verify that anyone working on your system holds a license through the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry lookup.

A seasonal checklist before the cold arrives

A little attention in September saves a bad night in January.

  • Set the thermostat and run the boiler for a full cycle
  • Check the pressure gauge and compare it to the manufacturer's range
  • Bleed radiators that are cold at the top
  • Look for drips around valves, pumps and fittings
  • Test your carbon monoxide detectors
