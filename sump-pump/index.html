Basement taking on water now

Every minute with a dead pump adds water to the floor.

  • Make sure the pump is plugged into a working outlet and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Lift the float by hand. If the pump runs, the float is stuck or tangled. If it does not run, the motor or switch has failed.
  • Move boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor and away from the pit.
  • Call the 24/7 line for same-day or after-hours dispatch. Do not step into standing water near outlets.

Where the water must go in South St. Paul

The City of South St. Paul requires sump pumps to discharge outside. The city does not allow sump pump discharge into the sanitary sewer, laundry tubs or floor drains. The Metropolitan Council makes the same point regionally, because clear water in the sanitary system overloads wastewater treatment.

If your pump drains into a floor drain or a laundry sink, we reroute it to a legal outdoor discharge. We also look at where the discharge ends so it does not freeze shut in January or flow back toward the foundation.

Floodwall, bluff and the basement

South St. Paul sits beside the Mississippi River, and the floodwall and levee were built in 1967 to protect much of the lowland area. The wall does not keep groundwater out of a basement. Groundwater and rain runoff do that on their own schedule.

Homes along the bluff above Concord Street face runoff more than river water. That is the case for a well-placed pit and a clear discharge. If your basement is damp without a pit, basement waterproofing explains the options.
