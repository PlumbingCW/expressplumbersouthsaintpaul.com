Main sewer cleaning versus drain cleaning

A drain cleaning call usually involves one fixture and a small cable. Main sewer line cleaning clears the pipe that carries everything out of the house, usually four inches wide and fifty feet or more. It takes a heavier machine, bigger cutters and often a camera.

If your question is one slow sink, start with drain cleaning. If the basement floor drain is bubbling when you run the washer, the main is the likely culprit.

How a main line cleaning goes

We follow the same order on every main.

Find the access

We locate the cleanout or pull a fixture if there is none.

Cut through

A heavy cable with a cutting head opens the blockage.

Scour and flush

Roots and grease get cut away, and hydro jetting finishes the job when the pipe needs it.

Look with a camera

We check the line afterward when a blockage was heavy or has returned.

What the city does and what you do

The City of South St. Paul maintains the sewer main under the street, and wastewater goes on to the Metropolitan Council's treatment system. Everything from your house to that main is private and belongs to you.

The city asks property owners to contact the Utilities Division before hiring a drain cleaner and to tell the city after cleaning a private line. Public Works is at 651-554-3225 on weekdays and 911 after hours, and we can help you sort out which side of the property line the blockage is on.
