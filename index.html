What South St. Paul plumbing is like

The City of South St. Paul runs its own water utility from municipal wells, and the city reports typical hardness around 18 grains per gallon. In practical terms that means scale in water heaters, clogged aerators and shorter tank life unless the heater is flushed.

The city's 2040 planning documents say about 74 percent of homes were built before 1970, with the oldest stock above the bluff west of Concord Street. Older houses often have cast iron or clay sewer lines and galvanized supply pipe. Our leak detection and sewer inspection pages explain how we check them without digging first.

Who owns which pipe

The sewer main in the street is the city's job, and the private line from your building to the main is yours. The city asks residents to contact its Utilities Division before hiring a drain cleaner and to report it after a private line is cleaned. Public Works answers at 651-554-3225 on weekdays and 911 after hours. Plumbing work in Minnesota follows Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

The water service line works the same way. The city's lead inventory counted about 740 lead lines and about 175 galvanized lines needing replacement as of October 2025, and private-side replacement may be done at no cost with a signed agreement. See water service line repair.
