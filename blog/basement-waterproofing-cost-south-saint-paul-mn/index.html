Basement Waterproofing Cost Estimate: South Saint Paul, MN Price Guide

A basement waterproofing estimate should start with a diagnosis, not a product. The fix for roof runoff at the foundation is not the fix for groundwater at the cove joint. We do not list a dollar range because no local source publishes one.

Here is what a good estimate lists and how to compare.

Cheaper fixes first

Many wet basements start with simple causes. Downspouts that dump at the wall, soil that slopes toward the house and window wells that fill up all send water toward the foundation. Fixing those costs far less than any interior system.

A good company tells you which of these apply before it recommends drain tile. If an estimate skips the outside entirely, ask why.

Local note: the floodwall and your basement

South St. Paul's floodwall and levee were built in 1967 and protect the area from roughly I-494 to just north of Wentworth Avenue. The National Weather Service gauge at St. Paul recorded crests of 26.01 feet in 1965 and 23.76 feet in 2001. Source type: city and National Weather Service records.

That wall addresses river flooding. It does not keep rain runoff or groundwater out of a basement, so it should not shape your waterproofing decision. Sump pump discharge must go outside under city rules, not to the sanitary sewer. See basement waterproofing for the services we offer.

Older homes and what to expect

The city's 2040 planning documents say about 74 percent of homes here were built before 1970. Older block or poured foundations can seep at mortar joints, wall cracks and the cove joint. These are typical in the pre-1960 houses that make up a large share of the city.

Newer homes, such as the few built since 2000, tend to have better drainage details, so problems there often point to grading or a pump issue. Mendota Heights and West St. Paul homeowners face similar questions.

Insurance and the estimate

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude seepage and groundwater. A water backup endorsement may cover a sump or sewer backup, but not the waterproofing itself. Read your policy before you assume coverage.

Compare at least two estimates. Ask each company to put the cause of the water, the method and the warranty terms in writing.

After the work: keep the basement dry

A pump needs a yearly test. A discharge line needs to be clear of ice and snow in winter. A dehumidifier keeps damp air from feeding mold, and a humidity reading of about half is a common target. Keep the downspouts extended and the window wells clean.

Waterproofing is a system, not a product. The best system fails if its pump fails or its discharge freezes, so ask about a battery backup and a discharge that drains away from the house.

Read the warranty before you read the price

Waterproofing warranties vary a great deal. Some cover the labor but not the pump. Some end if you sell the house, and some require yearly service visits. A transferable warranty can help when you sell, but only if you can read what it covers.

Ask what voids the warranty. A frozen discharge line, a power outage that stops the pump or a finished wall built over the drain can all create disputes. Ask for the terms in writing and ask who answers the phone when the pump fails at midnight.

Questions to bring to the first visit

Write down when water appears, how deep it gets and whether it follows rain, snowmelt or a high water table. Note any smell, any white mineral line on the wall and where the stains sit. This history helps a contractor tell surface runoff from groundwater in minutes.

Bring a copy of any earlier repair records, since a prior crack injection or a replaced pump changes the diagnosis. Ask the contractor to explain the cause in plain words before talking about price.
