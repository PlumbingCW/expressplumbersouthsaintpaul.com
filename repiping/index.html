Repiping in South Saint Paul, MN

If your house has pipes from the mid-century era, or you keep fixing new leaks, repiping replaces the supply lines instead of patching them one at a time. Call (855) 575-2890 and we will walk the house and explain the options.

Repiping is planned work. It is a fit for homes with low pressure, rusty water or repeat pinhole leaks, and we help you decide if it is time.

Why South St. Paul homes get repiped

The city's 2040 planning documents say about 74 percent of homes in South St. Paul were built before 1970. Homes of that period often used galvanized steel for water supply, and some had lead service lines or lead solder in copper joints.

Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside. The bore narrows, pressure drops and rust discolors the water. Repiping removes the failing pipe and replaces the supply network, from the main shutoff to the fixtures.

Materials compared

Each material has a place.

MaterialStrengthsWatch for
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, works well in retrofitsNot for outdoor exposure, and needs correct fittings
CopperLong track record, fire resistantHigher material cost, and aggressive water can pit it
CPVCLower cost, corrosion resistantCan become brittle with age and heat

Minnesota's plumbing code, chapter 4714, sets which materials are approved for potable water.

How a repipe is staged

A typical whole-house repipe runs in phases.

Survey

We trace the lines, find fixtures and plan the layout.

Rough-in

New lines go in through walls, floors and the basement ceiling.

Cutover

We shut water briefly and connect the new network.

Repair and test

We pressure test, then patch drywall openings or hand that off to your contractor.

Lead and the service line

Repiping the inside of the house does not replace the service line between the street main and your home. South St. Paul's lead inventory lists roughly 740 lead lines and about 175 galvanized lines needing replacement as of October 2025, and the city's program may cover the private side with a signed agreement.

If you plan to repipe, call the city first. Coordinating the service line and the house piping can save you from opening walls twice. See water service line repair for the outside piece.

How to get your house ready for a repipe

Small steps make the work go faster.

  • Clear access to the basement ceiling, closets and under-sink cabinets
  • Move furniture away from walls where pipe will be run
  • Plan where you will wash and cook during the water shutoff
  • Ask your plumber about drywall repair and who handles paint
  • Take photos of existing pipe locations for your records

Partial repipe versus whole house

Some homes only need the worst section replaced, such as the basement run or the kitchen branch. A partial repipe costs less and can be the right call if the rest of the pipe is sound. The risk is that old pipe elsewhere may leak next.

A whole-house repipe replaces the supply network at once and ends the repair cycle. We look at the pipe material, the leak history and the water quality, then tell you which fits the house.

What to do if a pipe leaks before the repipe

Do not wait for the project date if water is escaping.

  • Close the main water shutoff and open a low faucet to drain the lines
  • Place a bucket and towels under the leak and photograph the damage
  • Call the 24/7 line for same-day or after-hours repair
  • Tell your plumber a repipe is planned so the repair is kept small
