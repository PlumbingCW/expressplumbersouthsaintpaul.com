Hidden water running right now: what to do first

Hidden leaks do their damage slowly, until they do not.

  • Close the main shutoff, normally where the water line enters the house near the meter.
  • Turn off the water heater if you can see it is part of the leak, to protect the elements.
  • Move electronics and boxes away from wet walls and floors.
  • Call the 24/7 line. We schedule same-day and after-hours leak location for active water.

Test the meter before you call

Close every tap and appliance. Find the water meter, usually near where the line enters the house, and look for the small dial or triangle that spins when water is moving. If it spins with everything off, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

If the meter is still, the leak may be a dripping fixture or the problem may be on the hot side. Home meter tests do not replace a plumber, but they save everyone time and tell us whether the leak is inside or on the line outside.

Where leaks hide and how we look

Different spots need different methods. The method list below is how we match tool to location.

High water bill

A bill that jumps with no change in habits points to a running toilet, an irrigation line or a buried leak. A toilet flapper test and a meter test narrow it fast.

Slab and floor

Warm spots, a hum or rising bills can mean a leak under concrete. Acoustic listening and thermal imaging trace it without breaking the slab first.

Walls and ceilings

Staining, bubbling paint or a musty smell guide us to the wall cavity. Moisture readings and pressure tests tell us which line is affected.

Crawlspace and exterior walls

Frozen supply lines in unheated spaces are a Minnesota winter pattern. We check for splits and tell you whether heat tape or reroutes make sense.

Buried water line

A wet patch in the yard or a drop in pressure can mean a break between the meter and the house. See water service line repair.

Irrigation

Zones that stay wet, or a meter that moves with the system off, point to a leaking head or buried line.

Water that crosses the line from city to you

The City of South St. Paul runs its own water utility from municipal wells. The service line from the building to the main belongs to the property owner, so a leak between the main and your house is your repair and a leak in the street main is the city's.

The city's lead inventory lists hundreds of lead and galvanized service lines still in the system, and the program offers replacements. If your leak is on an older service line, a replacement may be smarter than a repair, so ask the city about the program before we dig.

What affects the cost of leak detection

Locating a leak is priced by time and method, then repair is quoted separately.

Method neededA meter test is simple. Acoustic, thermal and pressure methods take more time.

Size of the propertyA one-floor house costs less to survey than a multi-unit building.
Repair after detectionFinding the leak and fixing it are separate items, and we quote repairs once we know.
HoursEvenings and weekends carry an after-hours rate that we quote before dispatch.

Insurance often covers the water damage from a sudden leak but not the pipe itself or long-term seepage. Ask your carrier before you tear out drywall.
