Where faucet leaks come from

A drip from the spout is usually a worn cartridge, washer or seat. A leak at the base points to an O-ring. A leak under the sink often comes from a supply line, a compression fitting or the valve itself.

We name the part before we replace it, and we tell you when a faucet is worth saving. A budget faucet with a failed cartridge may cost more to repair than to swap.

Fixtures we see most

The faucet type decides the repair.

Single-handle kitchen faucet

Cartridge or ball seals wear out. Parts often fit by brand and model.

Two-handle bath faucet

Washers, seats or cartridges in each handle.

Tub and shower valve

A dripping shower can waste water for weeks. A valve repair may need access behind the wall. See leak detection if the wall is wet.

Outdoor spigot

Minnesota freezes split spigots from inside. A frost-proof sillcock helps.

Supply shutoff valve

Old stop valves seize or drip when turned. We replace them while we are there.

Do this first

Faucet spraying right now: what to do first

A failed valve can put water on the floor quickly.

  • Turn off the angle stop under the sink. If it does not stop the flow, close the main.
  • Open a lower faucet in the house to relieve pressure.
  • Put a bucket under the leak and keep towels on the cabinet floor.
  • Call the 24/7 line for same-day or after-hours dispatch.

Winter and outside faucets

Minnesota winters split unprotected spigots. A hose left attached traps water, and the freeze pushes the crack back into the wall. You may not see the leak until spring, when the spigot is opened and water enters the wall.

Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze. If you find water inside after turning on a spigot, shut the interior valve and call us. A split behind a wall is the case where burst pipe repair applies.
